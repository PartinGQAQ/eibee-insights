美/加披露规则更新：AI 内容与联盟链接都在监管范围内

美加监管 2026 年进入执法期：漏标披露单条罚款最高 53,088 美元，AI 内容需标注 #MadeWithAI，联盟链接逐帖披露。
发布 2026.11.11 期号 2026-11 篇幅 约 1,950 字 数据来源 6 份法规与评述

核心结论 Key Takeaways

  1. #Ad 单独使用是「金标准」披露词；#Gifted（产品置换）与 #InvitedGuest（受邀出席）被正式承认为有效披露。
  2. AI 虚拟红人须遵守与真人相同的披露义务并标注虚拟身份；AI 生成的产品画面需标注 #MadeWithAI。
  3. 联盟链接产生的佣金必须在每个含链接的帖子中单独披露。
  4. 行业自律之外还有法律兜底：加拿大《竞争法》对企业最高可罚 C$1,000 万或全球年营收 3%。

01加拿大：2025 年 10 月版指南的六个要点

加拿大广告自律机构 Ad Standards 于 2025 年 10 月发布新版《红人营销披露指南》，在 2018 年首版基础上补齐了 AI、联盟营销与儿童受众等新场景。面向加拿大市场投放的品牌应逐条核对：

要点具体要求
#Ad 为金标准单独使用、不与其他词组合，最易被观众理解
非现金利益免费产品（#Gifted）、活动邀请（#InvitedGuest）均构成实质性关联，需披露
AI 红人遵守与真人相同的披露义务，最佳实践是标注虚拟身份（#VirtualInfluencer / #AIinfluencer）
AI 画面AI 生成或显著修改的产品画面需标注（#MadeWithAI / #AIcreated）
联盟链接在每个包含链接的页面与帖子中标明（#affiliate / #CommissionEarned）
儿童受众披露需醒目、适龄、可理解；魁北克禁止面向 13 岁以下儿童的商业广告

另有两条实操变化值得注意：平台自带的「付费合作」标签若能做到显著、清晰、有效，可不再额外标注；拿不准时仍建议自行加 #Ad。同时披露位置要考虑「安全区」，确保桌面端与移动端都不会被界面元素遮挡。

#Ad标准披露词，需单独使用
#MadeWithAIAI 生成或修改画面的标注
U13魁北克禁止面向该年龄段投放广告

02别忽视法律兜底：《竞争法》罚则很重

Ad Standards 属行业自律，但《竞争法》为其提供强制性兜底：虚假或误导性广告对企业可处最高 1,000 万加元罚款（累犯 1,500 万加元）、违法所得三倍或全球年营收 3%（取最高者）；个人首次违规最高 75 万加元。加拿大竞争局 2026–27 年度计划已将 AI 相关误导营销列为重点关注领域。

换言之，AI 披露指南本身是自愿性行业规范，但「不得误导消费者」是法定义务——两者不在同一个层级上，后者可以直接开罚。

03美国市场：FTC 执法收紧，平台标签不再够用

美国没有红人营销专门立法，FTC《背书指南》仍是基准，但 2026 年执法明显收紧：漏标披露的民事罚款单条最高 53,088 美元，品牌与红人可被连带追责，AI 生成背书与深伪内容被列入年度执法重点。平台自带的付费合作标签不被视为充分披露，仍需在正文首行写明 #Ad / #Sponsored；AI 生成的推荐内容若可能让观众误以为真实体验，需明确标注，否则可能同时构成虚假背书。

04投放前合规清单

  • 合约中写明披露义务与具体标签（#Ad / #Gifted / #affiliate / #MadeWithAI）。
  • Brief 明确披露位置：标题或首行、各平台安全区内、桌面与移动端均可见。
  • 加拿大投放核查魁北克儿童广告限制；儿童向内容额外审查披露语言。
  • 联盟合作确保每个含链接的帖子都有独立披露。
  • 保留投放记录与内容快照，以备投诉处理与合规举证。
合规不是投放的终点文件，而是投放流程的一部分：披露写在合约里、标签写进 Brief、位置写进验收标准，才算真正落地。

Key Takeaways

  1. #Ad used alone is the gold-standard disclosure tag; #Gifted and #InvitedGuest are now officially recognized.
  2. AI virtual influencers follow the same disclosure duties as humans and should label their nature; AI-generated product imagery needs #MadeWithAI.
  3. Affiliate commissions must be disclosed on every post containing the link.
  4. Beyond self-regulation lies a legal backstop: Canada's Competition Act can fine corporations up to C$10 million or 3% of worldwide revenue.

01Canada: six takeaways from the October 2025 guidelines

Ad Standards, Canada's advertising self-regulatory body, released an updated Influencer Marketing Disclosure Guidelines in October 2025, adding AI, affiliate marketing and child-audience scenarios to the 2018 original. Brands running Canadian campaigns should check each item:

ItemRequirement
#Ad as gold standardUsed alone, not combined with other words, for maximum clarity
Non-cash benefitsFree products (#Gifted) and event invitations (#InvitedGuest) constitute material connections
AI influencersSame disclosure duties as humans; best practice is an identity label (#VirtualInfluencer / #AIinfluencer)
AI imageryAI-generated or significantly altered product imagery needs #MadeWithAI / #AIcreated
Affiliate linksDisclose on every page and post containing the link (#affiliate / #CommissionEarned)
Child audiencesDisclosures must be prominent, age-appropriate and explained; Quebec bans advertising to under-13s

Two practical changes matter: built-in paid-partnership labels suffice where they provide prominent, clear and effective disclosure; when in doubt, add #Ad yourself. Placement should also respect "safe zones" so disclosures stay visible on desktop and mobile alike.

#MadeWithAILabel for AI-generated or altered imagery

02Don't ignore the legal backstop: Competition Act penalties

Ad Standards is self-regulation, but the Competition Act provides a statutory backstop: false or misleading advertising can cost corporations up to C$10 million (C$15 million for repeat orders), three times the benefit derived, or 3% of annual worldwide revenue — whichever is greatest; individuals face up to C$750,000 on a first offence. The Competition Bureau's 2026–27 plan names AI-related deceptive marketing as a focus area.

In other words, the AI disclosure guidance is voluntary industry practice, while "do not mislead consumers" is statutory — the two sit on different levels, and the latter carries direct penalties.

03The US market: FTC enforcement tightens, platform tags no longer suffice

The US has no dedicated influencer-marketing statute, and the FTC's Endorsement Guides remain the baseline — but enforcement tightened markedly in 2026: civil penalties reach $53,088 per violation, brands and creators face joint liability, and AI-generated endorsements and deepfakes are named enforcement priorities. Built-in paid-partnership tags no longer count as adequate disclosure; #Ad or #Sponsored belongs in the first line of the caption. AI-generated endorsements that could pass as genuine experience must be labeled or risk constituting false endorsement.

04A pre-campaign compliance checklist

  • Write disclosure duties and exact tags into contracts (#Ad / #Gifted / #affiliate / #MadeWithAI).
  • Specify placement in briefs: caption opening, platform safe zones, visible on desktop and mobile.
  • Check Quebec's child-advertising restrictions for Canadian campaigns; review disclosure language for child-oriented content.
  • Ensure every affiliate post carries its own disclosure.
  • Keep campaign records and content snapshots for complaint handling and evidence.
Compliance is not a closing document but part of the process: disclosure in contracts, tags in briefs, placement in acceptance criteria.
