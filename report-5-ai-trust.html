AI 内容的信任账：平台在降权，观众要标注，品牌怎么办

YouTube 对量产内容取消变现资格，公众对 AI 内容的负面观感上升；AI 辅助与 AI 量产之间的边界，正在变成信任的分水岭。
发布 2026.11.11 期号 2026-11 篇幅 约 1,900 字 数据来源 4 份公开资料

核心结论 Key Takeaways

  1. 2025 年 7 月 15 日起，YouTube 把「重复内容政策」改为「不真实内容政策」：量产、模板化内容不可变现——针对的是量产，不是 AI 本身。
  2. 超过 40% 的 YouTube Shorts 已包含某种形式的 AI；平台要求对以假乱真的合成内容进行标注。
  3. 39% 的美国成年人认为 AI 弊大于利（18–29 岁达 47%），较一年前上升 8 个百分点。
  4. 62% 的加拿大人对「红人必须披露合作关系」感到安心——透明度是信任来源，不是负担。

01平台先动手：变现规则划出「量产线」

2025 年 7 月 15 日，YouTube 把「重复内容政策」升级更名为「不真实内容政策」：量产、模板化、缺乏原创观点的内容不再具备变现资格。需要注意，这不是对 AI 的禁令——用 AI 写脚本、做缩图、配音辅助的频道仍然可以变现；被划走的是「换了标题就再发一条」的模板化生产。

据公开报道，YouTube Shorts 中超过 40% 的内容已包含某种形式的 AI。平台同时要求创作者对以假乱真的合成内容进行标注，违者面临强制加标、下架或移出合作伙伴计划。

这条规则对品牌方的含义很直接：如果合作创作者的内容是流水线量产的，不仅观众不买账，连平台的变现资格都可能失去——你投放的长期价值会随内容质量一起清零。

02观众端：负面观感在累积，标注反而是加分项

公众情绪也在转向。盖洛普 2025 年 7 月的调查显示，39% 的美国成年人认为 AI 弊大于利，较一年前上升 8 个百分点，18–29 岁人群中这一比例达 47%。「AI slop」（AI 泔水内容）成为流行批评词，公众对低质生成内容的容忍度在快速下降。

但另一面同样成立：加拿大方面的调研显示，62% 的受访者对「创作者被要求披露合作关系」感到安心。透明并不削弱说服力，反而降低观众的抵触——这条结论对所有 AI 相关的披露都适用。

03规则收紧的三个具体方向

方向要求对品牌的影响
AI 身份虚拟红人须标注身份（#AIinfluencer / #VirtualInfluencer）虚拟人设可用于品牌自有内容，但不可伪装真人代言
AI 画面AI 生成或显著修改的产品画面须标注（#MadeWithAI）产品效果图、使用场景合成图需同步准备披露文案
代言边界AI 红人不得代言其无法亲身体验的属性（如味觉、气味）食品、美妆类目需特别注意，不能由虚拟形象做感官背书

美国 FTC 的背书指南对「实质性关联」的披露要求同样适用于 AI 生成的推荐内容；平台侧的合成内容标注规则与之并行，两者不互相替代。

04品牌的应对：把 AI 放在「辅助」位置

  • 签约前看生产方式：了解创作者的工作流，区分「AI 辅助的高质量创作」与「模板化量产」，后者一票否决。
  • Brief 里写明标注义务：涉及 AI 合成画面或虚拟形象的内容，标注要求写入合约，与披露义务同等对待。
  • 人设不可替代：真实体验、真人出镜、个人观点是信任来源，也是平台政策保护的对象。
  • 用 AI 提升运营而非制造内容：筛选、匹配、数据复盘交给 AI，创意与表达留给创作者。
信任是红人营销唯一的复利资产。AI 时代真正稀缺的，是「看起来依然像一个真实的人」的内容。

Key Takeaways

  1. Since July 15, 2025, YouTube's Repetitious Content policy became the Inauthentic Content policy: mass-produced, templated content is ineligible for monetization — aimed at mass production, not AI itself.
  2. Over 40% of YouTube Shorts already contain some form of AI; platforms require disclosure of realistic synthetic media.
  3. 39% of US adults believe AI does more harm than good (47% among 18–29s), up 8 points year over year.
  4. 62% of Canadians feel comforted that influencers must disclose brand relationships — transparency builds trust rather than eroding it.

01Platforms moved first: monetization draws the mass-production line

On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed its Repetitious Content policy to the Inauthentic Content policy: mass-produced, templated videos lacking original perspective are no longer eligible for monetization. This is not a ban on AI — channels using AI for scripts, thumbnails or voice assistance remain monetizable; what loses eligibility is template production that changes only the title.

Public reporting suggests over 40% of YouTube Shorts already contain some form of AI. Platforms also require creators to disclose realistic synthetic media, with enforcement including forced labeling, removal or suspension from the Partner Program.

For brands the implication is direct: if a partner creator's content is factory-produced, audiences won't buy it — and the content may even lose platform monetization, zeroing out the long-term value of your spend.

02The audience side: sentiment is souring, disclosure helps

Public sentiment is shifting. Gallup's July 2025 survey found 39% of US adults believe AI does more harm than good, up from 31% a year earlier, rising to 47% among 18–29s. "AI slop" has become a common criticism, and tolerance for low-quality generated content is falling fast.

The other side holds too: Canadian research shows 62% of respondents feel comforted that influencers are required to disclose brand relationships. Transparency does not weaken persuasion — it lowers resistance. That applies to AI-related disclosure as much as to ad disclosure.

03Three directions where rules are tightening

AreaRequirementImpact on brands
AI identityVirtual influencers must disclose their nature (#AIinfluencer / #VirtualInfluencer)Virtual personas work for owned content, but cannot pose as human endorsers
AI imageryAI-generated or significantly altered product imagery must be labeled (#MadeWithAI)Product result shots and synthetic usage scenes need matching disclosure copy
Endorsement limitsAI influencers cannot endorse attributes they cannot experience (taste, smell)Food and beauty categories need special care; no sensory claims from virtual personas

The US FTC's endorsement guides on material connections apply equally to AI-generated endorsements; platform-level synthetic-content labeling runs in parallel and does not replace them.

04What brands should do: keep AI in the assist seat

  • Vet the workflow before signing: distinguish AI-assisted quality creation from templated mass production; veto the latter.
  • Put labeling duties in the brief: make AI-synthetic disclosure contractual, treated the same as ad disclosure.
  • Persona is irreplaceable: real experience, real faces and personal viewpoints build trust and are what platform policies protect.
  • Use AI for operations, not content manufacturing: selection, matching and analytics belong to AI; creativity belongs to creators.
Trust is the only compounding asset in creator marketing. What is scarce in the AI era is content that still looks like it came from a real person.
